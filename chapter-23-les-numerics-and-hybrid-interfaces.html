23. LES numerics and hybrid interfaces

A hybrid calculation must do more than change the name of a viscosity. It must preserve momentum and energy exchanges while changing which fluctuations are represented, provide compatible information at an interface, and maintain adequate spatial and temporal resolution. The final numbered recording brings these decisions together: LES statistics, numerical dissipation, wall cost, zonal coupling and detached-eddy simulation.

Following information across representations. Begin with statistics of a filtered field and a correction to the familiar resolved-plus-modeled energy statement. Test central and upwind numerical amplification before judging their role in LES. Then derive conditional wall-cost scalings, construct an exact one-point inflow covariance, and inspect a versioned DES/DDES length switch. These checks expose the assumptions behind an otherwise simple hybrid diagram.

The rewrite retains its broad introduction while qualifying universal mesh rules, boundary conditions, fixed cost ratios and taxonomy. No DECIDER project report or benchmark data were inspected; the recording's favorable project report is not converted into independent validation. The runnable examples are deliberately restricted algebra and linear numerical analysis, not a new hybrid CFD computation.

23.1 Postprocessing LES requires two distinct averages L74 · 00:14

Let Fbe a deterministic linear spatial filter, \(v_i=Fu_i\) the exact filtered velocity and \(\mathbb E\) an ensemble expectation commuting with Funder appropriate integrability. The resolved fluctuation is \(v'_i=v_i-\mathbb E[v_i]\), with resolved Reynolds covariance \(R^r_{ij}=\mathbb E[v'_i v'_j]\). A numerical LES approximates v; computing a time mean from it also requires the stationarity and sampling considerations of chapters2and18. A single spatial bar is not a statistical mean.

The source writes turbulence energy as resolved plus modeled energy. That often-used interpretation needs a filter/mean qualification. Define \(U_i=\mathbb E[u_i]\), full covariance \(R_{ij}=\mathbb E[u_i u_j]-U_iU_j\), instantaneous central moment \(\tau_{ij}=F(u_i u_j)-(Fu_i)(Fu_j)\), and the filter moment of the mean \(\tau^U_{ij}=F(U_iU_j)-(FU_i)(FU_j)\). By direct addition and cancellation,

\[ F R_{ij}=R^r_{ij}+\mathbb E[\tau_{ij}]-\tau^U_{ij},\qquad F k=k_r+\mathbb E[k_s]-\frac12\tau^U_{ii}. \tag{23.1} \]

Here \(k=R_{ii}/2\), \(k_r=R^r_{ii}/2\) and \(k_s=\tau_{ii}/2\). The left side is the filtered full covariance/energy, not automatically the unfiltered point value. For a mean unchanged in the relevant quadratic filter sense, tauUvanishes and the simple addition holds. In a sheared mean, filtering a mean product differs from multiplying filtered means. This is the distinction developed in the two-level discussion of Germano1992, printed329–330, expressed here without assuming a spatially uniform mean.

For an exact constructed example, take \(u=(Sy,\xi,0)\), where the spatially constant random \(\xi\) has zero mean and variance0.04m²/s². A centered ybox preserves the linear mean and the fluctuation. With S100/sand Delta_y0.002m, tauU11=S²Delta_y²/12=0.00333333. The true filtered turbulent energy is0.02, resolved energy0.02and mean subfilter energy0.00166667. Naive addition gives0.02166667; subtracting the mean-filter contribution restores0.02. This local divergence-free example is an operator check, not a turbulent channel solution. In actual model postprocessing, the modeled tauis another approximation on top of(23.1).

23.2 Numerical dissipation is part of the represented dynamics L74 · 03:30

LES needs accurate transport of resolved fluctuations and a controlled transfer/loss near unresolved scales. Excessive artificial diffusion can suppress the very motions intended to be resolved; insufficient numerical control can permit an unstable computation. The source favors central schemes for explicitly modeled SGS calculations. That motivation concerns dissipation and dispersion, not a universal instruction that any centered spatial or temporal formula is stable.

Consider \(f_t+a f_x=0\), apositive, on a periodic interval, spacing h, time step dtand Courant number \(\gamma=a\,dt/h\). For mode \(e^{i\theta j}\), forward Euler with centered space has amplification \(g_c=1-i\gamma\sin\theta\), hence \(|g_c|^2=1+\gamma^2\sin^2\theta\). It grows except at special modes. Forward Euler with first-order upwind space has \(g_u=1-\gamma+\gamma e^{-i\theta}\), whose squared magnitude is \(1-2\gamma(1-\gamma)(1-\cos\theta)\); it is at most one for \(0\le\gamma\le1\), but damps modes.

At N64on a2pireference interval, mode3and gamma0.5,128steps represent one domain crossing. The centered-Euler amplitude grows to3.79710222while upwind-Euler decays to0.24833945, against exact amplitude1. A centered-space Crank–Nicolson time step instead has amplification \((1-i\gamma\sin\theta/2)/(1+i\gamma\sin\theta/2)\), of unit magnitude. It still has phase error, and this linear result is not a nonlinear LES stability proof. Temporal treatment and spatial treatment must be assessed together.

A modified-equation expansion for the fully discrete forward-Euler upwind scheme gives leading artificial viscosity \(\nu_{\rm num}=ah(1-\gamma)/2\), here0.02454369in reference units. The corresponding continuous-time upwind spatial operator has leading ah/2instead; the distinction matters. Neither number should be added blindly to a nonlinear turbulent model's coefficient as if they were identical tensors at all scales.

The recording introduces MILES, monotonically integrated LES, in which selected numerical regularization supplies unresolved-scale control without a separate conventional SGS viscosity. This is a deliberate implicit-LES strategy, not a claim that every upwind calculation automatically models turbulence well. Spalart's 2001 DES guide, inspected opening discussion, explicitly discusses interaction between numerical dissipation and model constants. Its historical observations are not a current hardware or universal algorithm recommendation. Adding explicit SGS dissipation and strong artificial diffusion can overdamp, while mixing them does not mathematically guarantee failure either: examine the actual discrete energy budget.

23.3 Wall resolution is a physical and numerical choice L74 · 07:00

Domain-size, initialization and correlation checks from DNS remain relevant to LES. Small half-box correlations are useful warnings, with the periodic and anisotropy qualifications from chapter18. They do not prove that a box containing an asserted “two eddies” is adequate. A coarse filter estimate based on etaalso does not determine an adequate LES mesh. Choosing every spacing10or100timesetato save cost says nothing by itself about the retained shear structures, filter location or quantity of interest. Grid sensitivity must include the model/representation changes explained in chapter19.

In wall-resolved LES the first wall-normal point is commonly designed in the viscous region, with \(y^+\)of order1or less, while the two tangential spacings are chosen for elongated structures and the numerical scheme. The source's illustrative tangential values around100and30in wall units are estimates, not a complete universal resolution prescription. First-point yplusalone does not control wall gradients or the entire energy spectrum. In wall-modeled LES, a wall model supplies stress or flux using information away from the unresolved inner region; its assumptions, sampling height and coupling need verification.

Predicting drag or heat transfer does not logically require wall-resolved LES in every problem. Those quantities can be modeled, with associated error and applicability limits. Conversely, a model used for atmospheric roughness is not automatically a model for arbitrary flame quenching or thermal surface flux. This chapter makes no combustion or heat-transfer recommendation beyond the need for the appropriate equations and wall closure. Physical pressure-gradient conditions must follow momentum and boundary compatibility; velocities/pressure are not universally Dirichlet/Neumann at every boundary.

23.4 Cost exponents require their Reynolds number and grid assumptions L74 · 13:40

The source compares approximately linear wall-modeled point demand with near-quadratic wall-resolved demand. A bounded primary basis is Choi and Moin's2011 CTR report, printed31–34, the precursor to the2012article. It distinguishes a flat-plate length Reynolds number \(Re_{L_x}=U_\infty L_x/\nu\) from the turbulent integral Reynolds number in homogeneous DNS and retains assumptions about nested grids and leading-edge location. Its estimates are not interchangeable exponents for any geometry.

For example, fix plate dimensions and tangential spacings in wall units. The wall-layer point density per surface area scales as \(u_\tau^2/\nu^2\), for a fixed number of normal points in the selected wall range. If skin-friction coefficient \(c_f=2u_\tau^2/U_\infty^2\) scales like \(Re_x^{-1/7}\), then integrating over a comparable streamwise range gives \(N_{\rm wr}\)scaling like \(Re_{L_x}^{13/7}\), approximatelypower1.857. This recovers the source's rough1.85with its conditions. A tenfold Reynolds increase gives a point multiplier71.9686under this idealized family, before time steps and observation duration.

For a wall-modeled grid with directional spacings proportional to local boundary-layer thickness \(\delta(x)\), point count scales as \(\int_{x_0}^{L_x}L_z\,\delta(x)^{-2}dx\). With \(\delta\propto(\nu/U_\infty)^{1/7}x^{6/7}\), this is proportional to \((U_\infty/\nu)^{2/7}[x_0^{-5/7}-L_x^{-5/7}]\). If the inlet Reynolds number \(U_\infty x_0/\nu\) remains fixed as nu decreases, x0also decreases and the leading dependence becomes \(Re_{L_x}\). If instead x0/Lxis fixed, the dependence is \(Re_{L_x}^{2/7}\). An exponent requires the family of problems, not just a slogan about LES.

The homogeneous-cube DNS estimate \(Re_L^{9/4}\)from chapter18uses another definition and grid hypothesis. The inspected flat-plate report instead obtains37/14for its uniform cross-plane DNS estimate and explicitly notes omitted nested/stretched refinement. Yang and Griffin's2021 revision, abstract and introduction, revisits that grid assumption and reports a different DNS scaling when local spacings follow local dissipative lengths. This is enough to reject a universal cost exponent; no full later-paper proof is claimed here. Point counts alone also omit time-step, solver, communication and statistical cost. The source's illustrative “a few times RANS” DESwall-clock ratio is not a general performance guarantee.

23.5 Similar equation shapes do not identify the represented field L74 · 16:30

RANS evolves ensemble-mean quantities with modeled Reynolds stresses; LES evolves a filtered unsteady field with modeled subfilter stress. Their incompressible momentum equations can have similar differential forms under scalar-viscosity closure. A hybrid approach uses these treatments in selected regions or through a coupled model. But switching a coefficient alone does not specify the physical meaning of the field, turbulence content, model-state transfer or adequate mesh/time integration.

A zonal or embedded approach assigns an LESregion and a RANSregion, sometimes on separate or overlapping meshes. Separate meshes are one implementation, not the definition of every hybrid method. A non-zonal formulation such as DESuses a common field and model length or blending/shielding rule to change behavior. Terminology varies across formulations, so record the actual equations and coupling rather than assume one universal taxonomy.

The averaged equations can retain a time derivative. “URANS”normally emphasizes their unsteady numerical use, in contrast with a steady RANSsolution. Coherent time dependence in such a solution does not imply the full turbulent spectrum is resolved as in LES. A mathematically unsteady ensemble mean and a filtered realization remain different objects. Both RANSand LEScan have multiple regimes or initial-condition sensitivity; neither method name ensures uniqueness.

23.6 A RANS-to-LES interface needs more than a mean profile L74 · 23:35

Suppose a RANSregion supplies mean Uand a PSDtarget covariance R. An LESinflow needs a compatible evolving velocity, schematically \(v=U+\xi\), where the synthetic fluctuation has zero mean and appropriate covariance, spatial/time correlations and boundary compatibility. Matching one-point covariance is necessary for that target but does not determine a divergence-free field or a realistic spectrum. Interpolation must also respect mass flux and the interface's momentum transfer.

Our recurring covariance has entries R11=0.25,R22=0.09,R33=0.04,R12=−0.08,others0in squared meters per squared second. Let a,b,cbe independent equiprobable signs. Set \(\xi_1=0.5a\), \(\xi_2=-0.16a+\sqrt{0.09-0.16^2}\,b\), \(\xi_3=0.2c\). All eight sign triples have zero exact ensemble mean and produce the target R. This is a Cholesky-type moment construction. It supplies no spatial derivative or time correlation, so it cannot by itself be advertised as complete LESinflow. Adding it independently at each cell can introduce divergence or inappropriate high-frequency content.

In the reverse direction, an LEScan supply averaged resolved covariance and an estimate of unresolved covariance, with the mean-filter correction in(23.1)where applicable. A scalar reconstruction \(k_{\rm RANS}=Ck_r\)has \(C=1/f_r\)only if the resolved fraction \(f_r=k_r/k_{\rm RANS}\)is known and the definitions are compatible. An illustrative kr0.12plus unresolved0.07gives C1.583333; another filter or location gives another fraction. One fitted multiplier cannot reconstruct all anisotropic stresses or dissipation. Interface studies should compare total transfer and stress, not only the appearance of resolved vortices.

23.7 A model length can trigger an unintended change of behavior L74 · 26:45

One original SA-based DESidea replaces a wall-distance destruction length dby \(\widetilde d=\min(d,C_{\rm DES}\Delta_D)\), where Delta_Dis the model's selected grid measure. Taking a maximum cell dimension is a common original-grid interpretation; it is not necessarily the cube-root volume used in a Smagorinsky example. The actual Foundation4.1 dTilda function computes the minimum using its configured Delta. The complete SAclosure and grid-scale selection are further parts of that implementation; we are inspecting the length mechanism alone.

When dsmaller, the RANSlength remains active; when the grid-based scale is smaller, the model length is reduced toward an LES-like scale. This aims to retain modeled attached boundary-layer stresses and resolve larger separated motions where the mesh supports them. “Detached”does not mean every point geometrically above a boundary-layer edge is a turbulent LESregion, nor that all versions guarantee the entire attached layer remains RANS. Transition regions and resolved turbulence development matter.

For d0.01m,Cdes0.65and Delta_D0.02m, the minimum is d. Refining Delta_Dto0.005mreduces the length to0.00325m. If this occurs inside an attached layer without adequate resolved stress replacing modeled stress, total stress can fall. This is modeled-stress depletion; the resulting altered separation can be grid-induced. The issue is not simply that “finer is worse,” but that refinement has changed the closure regime before the physical content and representation can support it.

23.8 Shielding and the limits of a hybrid label L74 · 29:30

A delayed/shielded form can write \(\widetilde d=d-f_d\max(0,d-C_{\rm DES}\Delta_D)\), with0≤fd≤1. At fd0it retains d; at fd1it recovers the unshielded minimum. The inspected Foundation4.1 DDES functions compute a dimensionless ratio from effective viscosity, gradient magnitude and squared kappad, use \(f_d=1-\tanh[(8r_d)^3]\), and apply a positive length floor. This is a specific version, not every later DDESor IDDESmodel.

For nonnegative effective viscosity and dpositive, the ratio is nonnegative; large rdpushes fdtoward0and small rdtoward1. Our code checks fdfor rd0.01,0.1,1and prescribed protected/unprotected lengths. It does not diagnose a boundary layer from actual velocity data or reproduce the whole SAtransport model. A computed switch and positive length do not demonstrate resolved turbulent content. Shielding can also delay the appearance of resolved structures; model variants address different compromises.

The completed numbered course therefore leaves a practical research task, not an automatic best-model rule: define the prediction, select equations/variant and boundary treatment, verify implementation and discrete balances, study spatial/time/domain/statistical sensitivity, then compare against independent physical evidence with uncertainty. The live-session chapters revisit such choices through the questions actually raised by the audience.

"""Advection amplification, statistical partition and hybrid-interface \
algebra."""

from itertools import product
from math import cos, pi, sin, sqrt, tanh

n, m, cfl = 64, 3, 0.5
h = 2 * pi / n
theta = 2 * pi * m / n
steps = int(n / cfl)
central_gain = sqrt(1 + (cfl * sin(theta)) ** 2)
upwind_gain = sqrt((1 - cfl + cfl * cos(theta)) ** 2 + (cfl * sin(theta)) ** 2)
print(
    f"forward Euler centered/upwind amplitude after one crossing: "
    f"{central_gain ** steps:.8f}, {upwind_gain ** steps:.8f}"
)
print(
    f"leading forward-Euler upwind numerical viscosity at unit speed: "
    f"{h * (1 - cfl) / 2:.8f}"
)
print("centered-space Crank-Nicolson linear-advection amplitude: 1.00000000")

shear, dy, fluct_variance = 100.0, 0.002, 0.04
mean_filter_stress = shear**2 * dy**2 / 12
resolved_k = fluct_variance / 2
mean_q = mean_filter_stress / 2
true_filtered_k = resolved_k
corrected = resolved_k + mean_q - mean_filter_stress / 2
print(
    f"filtered turbulence energy, naive resolved+SGS, corrected: "
    f"{true_filtered_k:.10f}, {resolved_k + mean_q:.10f}, "
    f"{corrected:.10f}"
)

# Eight equiprobable signs exactly realize this one-point target covariance.
rows = []
for a, b, c in product((-1.0, 1.0), repeat=3):
    rows.append((0.5 * a, -0.16 * a + sqrt(0.09 - 0.16**2) * b, 0.2 * c))
means = [sum(row[i] for row in rows) / len(rows) for i in range(3)]
cov = [
    [sum(row[i] * row[j] for row in rows) / len(rows) for j in range(3)]
    for i in range(3)
]
print(f"synthetic one-point fluctuation mean: {means}")
print(f"synthetic one-point covariance: {cov}")
print(
    f"synthetic one-point energy: {sum((cov[i][i] for i in range(3))) / 2:.8f}"
)
assert abs(cov[0][1] + 0.08) < 1e-14 and abs(cov[1][1] - 0.09) < 1e-14
print(
    f"energy-feedback multiplier for one illustrative partition "
    f".12+.07: {(0.12 + 0.07) / 0.12:.8f}"
)

cd = 0.65
for d, grid, fd in (
    (0.01, 0.02, 1.0),
    (0.01, 0.005, 1.0),
    (0.01, 0.005, 0.0),
    (0.05, 0.005, 1.0),
):
    des = min(d, cd * grid)
    delayed = d - fd * max(0.0, d - cd * grid)
    print(
        f"wall distance={d}, grid length={grid}, fd={fd}: DES "
        f"length={des:.8f}, shielded length={delayed:.8f}"
    )
for rd in (0.01, 0.1, 1.0):
    fd = 1 - tanh((8 * rd) ** 3)
    print(f"shielding rd={rd}: fd={fd:.10f}")
for label, power in (
    ("WM illustrative", 1.0),
    ("WR high-Re plate", 13 / 7),
    ("homogeneous DNS", 9 / 4),
    ("uniform-cross-plane plate DNS", 37 / 14),
):
    print(
        f"tenfold Reynolds ratio, {label}: point multiplier={10 ** power:.8f}"
    )

23.9 Exercises with complete solutions

1. Prove the general covariance partition.

By definitions Rr=E[(Fu)(Fu)]−(FU)(FU)and Etau=F E(uu)−E[(Fu)(Fu)]. Their sum is F E(uu)−(FU)(FU). Subtract tauU=F(UU)−(FU)(FU)to obtain F[E(uu)−UU]=FR. Half tracing gives the energy statement. If tauUnonzero, resolved plus mean SGSenergy includes filtering of the sheared mean, not only turbulent covariance. An approximate SGSmodel adds error beyond this exact operator identity.

2. Derive the two advection amplification factors.

Substitute f_j^n=g^n exp(i theta j)into forward-Euler centered advection to get gc=1−i gamma sin theta. Its squared norm exceeds1for a nonzero sine. Upwind gives gu=1−gamma+gamma exp(−i theta), squared norm1−2gamma(1−gamma)(1−cos theta), stable in this norm for0≤gamma≤1. This second scheme damps; stability does not imply accurate turbulence. The Crank–Nicolson centered ratio has conjugate numerator/denominator and unit magnitude, with remaining phase error.

3. Derive the leading fully discrete upwind artificial viscosity.

Expand the forward difference in time as f_t+dt f_tt/2and backward difference in space as f_x−h f_xx/2. Using the leading advection equation gives f_tt=a²f_xx. Rearrangement yields \(f_t+af_x=[ah/2-a²dt/2]f_{xx}+\cdots\), so nu_num=ah(1−gamma)/2. It depends on both space and time discretization. A continuous-time upwind spatial operator lacks the temporal correction and has ah/2instead.

4. State the family behind the wall-cost exponent.

At fixed positive wall-unit tangential spacings and a specified number of normal wall-layer points, local surface point density scales as u_tau²/nu². With fixed external geometry and cfproportional to Rex^−1/7, its integration gives the conditional ReLx^(2−1/7)=ReLx^(13/7)dependence. Wall-modeled delta^-2integration depends on x0: keeping inlet Rex0fixed produces leading ReLx, whereas fixed x0/Lxproduces ReLx^(2/7). Constants, normal grids, time steps and statistics remain separate costs.

5. Verify the synthetic covariance and identify missing information.

Independent signs have means0,variances1and zero cross moments. Thus E[xi1²]=.25, E[xi2²]=.16²+(.09−.16²)=.09, E[xi3²]=.04and E[xi1xi2]=.5(−.16)=−.08. Other cross terms vanish. Half trace is.19. The construction specifies only one-point ensemble moments; it supplies no divergence-free spatial extension, wall behavior, integral length or time correlation. Those must be generated and checked for an actual inflow.

6. Why is a scalar LES-to-RANS energy multiplier conditional?

If total k=kr+kmwith compatible filter/mean definitions and fr=kr/kknown, then k=kr/frand C=1/fr. For kr.12,km.07, C=.19/.12=1.583333. Changing the unresolved fraction changes C. With a sheared mean use the correction in(23.1); with signed filters even naive positivity arguments need care. No scalar kconversion reconstructs all six stress entries or a dissipation scale.

7. Derive the shielded-length limits.

If d≤CdesDelta, max(0,d−CdesDelta)=0, so length is dfor any fd. If d>CdesDelta, fd1gives CdesDeltaand fd0gives d. For0≤fd≤1it is a convex combination of those two positive lengths. This algebra proves the limiting length mechanism, not correct region identification. The actual fdcalculation, grid measure and model transport must be specified to predict a flow.

8. Explain how refinement can expose modeled-stress depletion.

At fixed d, reducing Delta_Dcan switch the minimum from dtoCdesDelta_Dand reduce the modeled transport length. If resolved fluctuations have not developed or the mesh cannot support them, modeled stress declines without corresponding resolved stress. The total momentum transport then changes and can alter separation. Shielding aims to retain RANSbehavior in that situation. A test must inspect total stress, resolved fluctuations, mesh/time/domain dependence and independent observables; a switch flag or favorable wall-clock ratio alone cannot validate it.
